# Halftone Media — BRIEF

Interviewed, four questions, one pass. Answers below are the user's decisions;
everything marked *authored* is mine under the delegation implied by "sleek
modern, use your judgment on the rest."

## 1. Vibe, three to five words
**Bold editorial, high contrast.** User's pick from the offered range. Hard
light/dark cuts mid-page, louder type, more attitude, still clean.
References (*authored*): a broadcast slate; the black-and-bone of a Pentagram
annual report; the moment a cable break cuts from programme to black.

## 2. The scroll journey, section by section
*Authored.* The user asked for a pitch page plus a partner list. The journey:
open on the admission that the clients are not famous, name what a break is,
run the roster past at speed, land the one large name plainly, say what the shop
actually does, go quiet, then the peak, then the ask.

## 3. The energy curve
*Authored.* Loud open, loud roster, a genuine silence at act ten, the peak at
eleven, abrupt close. The silence is authored, not dead scroll: see below.

## 4. Feeling curve, and the one moment

One line per act. Emotion first, cause second. Twelve acts, nothing held,
nothing over 1.3 viewport-heights. [E1..E6] marks which act hosts which
exposure of the rotation.

```
1   Jolt           black, the admission, and it cuts to bone inside one screen
2   Recognition    the break named plainly, on the first hard cut          [E1]
3   Doubt          one small question on a very wide empty ground
4   Curiosity      the four lengths at true relative width, wiped in       [E2]
5   Confidence     what the shop is, stated without a single image         
6   Understanding  the six things it does, set as a table, not as cards    [E3]
7   Appetite       the roster going past faster than it can be read
8   Weight         one large name, alone on a screen, undecorated
9   Trust          how a spot is actually made, fact not pitch             [E4]
10  Quiet          a whole empty screen. AUTHORED SILENCE, not dead scroll [E5]
11  Awe            the sixth exposure takes the screen and names the trick [E6]
12  Resolve        abrupt, full bleed, the accent owns the region, the ask
```

No two adjacent acts carry the same feeling. The one act of real quiet sits
directly in front of the peak, which is what makes the peak a change rather
than one more loud screen.

**The peak: act 11.** The sentence a visitor says to a friend:

> the same line had been following me down the whole page and then it went
> full screen and told me it was the sixth time

**Authored silence: act 10.** One bone screen, a single label at the top edge,
no motion. Deliberate. Recorded here so a verification pass can tell it from
dead scroll.

## The score

Four device families, never the same one twice running, no pinning anywhere.

| Act | Device | Why this one |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Jolt | `in`, short stagger | The grammar opens mid sentence. No settling shot |
| 2 Recognition | hard ground cut + `in` | A cut is the subject of the page |
| 3 Doubt | scale contrast alone | Type at reading size on an empty ground is the quietest device there is |
| 4 Curiosity | `reveal` (left) | A wipe across the strip is the break filling, left to right |
| 5 Confidence | `in`, staggered | The one act that reads like a document, which is why it belongs here |
| 6 Understanding | `in` on a table | Plain typeset fact. Cards here would be the machine-made tell |
| 7 Appetite | marquee | Going past faster than you can read it is the honest shape for the list |
| 8 Weight | `in`, trail anchor | One name, one screen, nothing else on it |
| 9 Trust | `in`, lead anchor | Prose, because this beat is a procedure |
| 10 Quiet | nothing | The absence is the device |
| 11 Awe | the rotation, in chrome | The grammar bans `pin`, so the peak lives outside the act stack |
| 12 Resolve | `in` + accent ground | The accent owns one region of the page rather than being scattered |

## 5. One thing no other site does
*Authored, and it is the signature move.* **The frequency rotation.** Television
works by repetition: a spot earns its money on the fifth or sixth exposure, not
the first. So the page does to the reader what the medium does to a viewer. One
line, "The sixth time is the one that works", is planted six times down the
page at six different scales, positions and grounds, from a fixed chrome layer
that outlives every act. A small readout in the chrome counts the exposures
honestly. At the sixth it takes the whole screen and the page says what it did.
The argument is not made, it is performed on the reader.

## 6. Distance from premium-minimal
User: **bold editorial, high contrast.** Not premium-minimal, not maximalist.

## 7. One unbroken world, or distinct scenes
**Distinct scenes, hard cut.** Follows from the grammar and from the subject: a
commercial break is a sequence of hard cuts, and interpolating between them
would be a lie about the medium.

## 8. Assets already held
**None.** No footage, no photography, no logo, no brand kit. Confirmed by the
absence of any asset in the request.

Consequence, decided here rather than discovered later: **zero generated
assets, zero spend, no video on the page.** The typography, the grounds and the
broadcast graphic vocabulary (slate, colour bars, timecode, slot rules) carry
the whole page. `scrub` is therefore unavailable, which the chosen grammar bans
at length anyway, so nothing is lost.

## The tell-someone sentence

> It's the site that runs its own slogan at you six times and then tells you
> that is the entire business.

## Grammar, and why the other seven lost

**Rhythmic cutlist.**

| Grammar | Why it lost |
|---|---|
| Filmic one-shot | Carries the visitor where an ad house should be demonstrating the cut. Also the default four prior builds drifted into, and it carries a burden of proof the brief does not meet |
| Chaptered editorial | Built for long-form substance. A reel is not a feature article |
| Live surface | There is no running product to put on screen |
| Continuous world | Needs a generated flight. No assets, no budget, and the seamlessness would contradict the subject |
| Typographic poster | Closest runner-up, and the scale contrast is borrowed. Lost because it forbids the structure a partner roster needs |
| Gallery / catalog | Correct for the roster, wrong for the pitch. Used on page two instead |
| Split stage | No two-sided argument to resolve |

## Real numbers

There are none, so there are no counters. The one counter on the page counts
the reader's own exposures to the rotation line, which is a fact about the page
itself and verifiable by scrolling it.
